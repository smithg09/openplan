Incident explainer

How a retry storm took down checkout

An eleven-minute database lock should have cost us eleven minutes. It cost us ninety-three, because our clients tried to be helpful.

410 ms → 3.9 sp95 checkout latency
6.2%of sessions abandoned the cart
93 minfrom first alert to recovery

What happened

At 21:08 UTC a schema migration on the orders table took a lock and held it for eleven minutes. Payment authorisations queued behind it. Every failed call was retried, and the retry policy was three attempts with no delay between them, so the queue grew faster than the database could ever drain it.

When the lock released at 21:19, every client that had been waiting fired its pending retries at the same moment. That synchronised burst, not the lock itself, is what kept checkout down until 22:41.

What we are changing

Click an item to mark it done as the work lands.

  • ✓Cap payment retries at two attempts, with exponential backoff and full jitter.
  • ✓Run schema changes through the online-migration tool only; block anything that touches orders without a dry run on a production-sized replica.
  • ✓Add a load-shedding switch that returns a fast 503 once the queue passes a threshold.
  • ✓Move the on-call rota to a follow-the-sun pattern from 1 December.
